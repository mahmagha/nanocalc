## How to review

This sheet is for a scientist with expertise in nanomaterials characterization. For every item, please check it against the cited source and tick it, adding your initials and the date. Note problems inline or open an issue.

1. **Formulas.** Each equation as the app displays it (plain text and LaTeX). Check it matches the cited source, including the definitions of symbols, units and any numerical prefactor.
2. **Default parameters and presets.** Check that typical values (densities, wavelengths, shape factors, empirical coefficients) are correct and that their source is appropriate.
3. **References.** Check that each citation exists, the bibliographic details and DOI are correct, and the source actually supports the use made of it.
4. **Validity limits.** In the app, open the calculator and read its warnings and notes. Check that the stated range of validity matches the literature.

Run the app (`node tools/serve.js`, then http://localhost:8080) to see each formula in context with worked numbers, and `node tests/run-tests.js` for the automated checks against known values.

### BJH pore-size distribution (`bjh`)

Steps down the isotherm from high to low pressure. In each interval the newly emptied pores are sized with the Kelvin equation plus the film thickness, and their volume is corrected for the film that thins in pores emptied earlier (Barrett, Joyner & Halenda 1951). Gives the pore volume and area in each size class, dV/dD, and the totals.

**Formulas** (11 distinct, from 5 input branches)

- [ ] **Liquid volume adsorbed**: `V_liq = V_ads · M / (ρ_L · V_mol)`  
  $V_{\text{liq}} = V_{\text{ads}}\,\dfrac{M}{\rho_L V_{\text{mol}}}$
  Cites: Lowell 2004. — verified by ________ date ________
- [ ] **Kelvin radius at each point**: `r_K = −2γ·V_L / [R·T·ln(p/p₀)],   V_L = M/ρ_L`

  Cites: Thomson 1871; Lowell 2004. — verified by ________ date ________
- [ ] **Film thickness and pore radius**: `t [nm] = [0.1399 / (0.034 − log₁₀(p/p₀))]^½;   r_p = r_K + t`  
  $t [\text{nm}] = [0.1399 / (0.034 - \log _{10}(p/p_{0}))]^{1/2};\quad r_{p} = r_{K} + t$

## Spectrum & peak analysis

### Spectrum import & peak fitting (`peakfit`)

Paste or upload a two-column spectrum (x, y). NanoCalc can smooth it (Savitzky–Golay) and subtract a linear, polynomial or Shirley baseline. It then finds the peaks and fits them all together with Gaussian, Lorentzian or pseudo-Voigt profiles by Levenberg–Marquardt. You get positions, widths (FWHM) and areas with standard errors, plus tables ready to paste into the Scherrer, Williamson–Hall and XPS calculators.
